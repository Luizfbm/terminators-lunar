# The Uncertainty band comes from DEM clones raised and lowered by NASA's per-pixel height error

Status: proposed

ADR 0003 promises an Uncertainty band made by perturbing the DEM. This ADR fixes how, so the number and the way it is made can be checked by someone who was not here. `GET /visibility/uncertainty` is the route; the contract is in `contract/openapi.yaml`.

## Decision

**What a clone is.** For each DEM band of the Horizon mask that has a per-pixel error product, a clone adds `σ · z` to the heights, where `σ` is the product's own height error in metres (the PGDA `LDEM_*_ADJ_ERR` raster, one 1σ value per pixel, on the same grid as the DEM) and `z` is a spatially correlated standard normal field: unit variance, zero mean, the same field for the whole band's window. Pixels outside the DEM are never touched. The Site's own height is read from the clone, so the Observer stands on the perturbed terrain. Only the Horizon mask changes: the Sun's and the Earth's positions do not depend on terrain, so one ephemeris scan per Query is shared by every clone (the Observer is placed at the nominal Site height; a metre of height moves the Sun's direction by about 1e-9 rad). Every clone then goes through the same event and metric code as the nominal `/visibility` run, so a clone with `σ = 0` gives the nominal answer to the second.

**Which DEMs.** PGDA publishes a height error raster for every polar DEM the engine uses: `LDEM_80S_20MPP_ADJ_ERR`, `LDEM_80S_80MPP_ADJ_ERR` and `LDEM_60S_240MPP_ADJ_ERR` (also `LDEM_60S_60MPP_ADJ_ERR`, 4.2 GB, which only Chandrayaan-3's off-pole crop would use). The off-pole Curated Sites are `uncovered` for now: SLDEM2015 (Apollo 11, Blue Ghost 1) has no published per-pixel error, and the Chandrayaan-3 crops are cut by `deploy/crop_offpole.py`, which does not cut the error product yet. A band is made from the bands that are covered; the others are listed in `uncovered` and their heights are not varied. A Query with no covered band gets a band of width zero from one clone, `clones: 1`, and `error_products: []`: the answer is the nominal one and says nothing about the map's error, and `uncovered` says why.

**Correlation of `z`.** The field is white noise smoothed with a Gaussian kernel, rescaled to unit variance, so its correlation at distance `r` is `exp(-(r/ℓ)²)` and `ℓ` is where it falls to 1/e. We take `ℓ = 20 m` for every band. The source is Barker et al. (2021, Planetary and Space Science 203, 105119; the LEAG 2021 poster and the 2023 paper that describes the `_ERR` products): the error of a gridded height is dominated by interpolation across the gaps between LOLA spots and tracks, it "is spatially correlated primarily with gap size", and the gap size is what they call the effective resolution, the diameter of the largest circle that fits between spots. The 2023 paper says "much of the Artemis zone (i.e., poleward of 84° S) has an effective resolution of ~20 m", and the `_ERR` rasters are the clones' RMS error fitted against that quantity. We measured it on the PGDA `_EFFRES` rasters around Curated Site 001: median 14.5 m in the 0-5 km ring (20 m DEM), 19.2 m in the 5-100 km ring (80 m DEM) and 38.6 m in the 100-300 km ring (240 m DEM). So 20 m is the documented and measured scale, and it is not tuned to any result. Consequence to keep in mind: against pixels of 20, 80 and 240 m, `ℓ` is 1, 1/4 and 1/12 of a pixel, so at 80 and 240 m neighbouring pixels are in practice independent, and only the 20 m band has a correlation you can see. What the model does not have is the long-wavelength part of the real error (the orbit's geolocation residuals, about 1 m along and across the tracks per Barker 2023, which make errors that run for kilometres along a track). We have no published length for it, so we do not invent one; the band therefore describes the pixel-scale error NASA publishes, not every possible error. `LUNAR_UNCERTAINTY_CORRELATION_M` changes `ℓ` for a sensitivity study; the cache key holds it.

**How many clones.** 32 by default (`LUNAR_UNCERTAINTY_CLONES`, 1 to 100). Barker et al. used 100 for a whole-DEM study. For a 5th or 95th percentile of `N` independent clones the standard error is about `σ·sqrt(p(1-p)/N) / φ(z_p)`, with `p` the percentile and `φ(z_p)` the standard normal density at it: 0.37σ for 32 clones and 0.21σ for 100 (`σ` is the spread of that quantity across clones). The edge of the band at 32 therefore moves by roughly a third of the band's own spread from one seed to another. That is enough to read the order of magnitude and the side of the band, not a second decimal, and it keeps a band within the minutes a background job can take on the VPS (each clone is one engine slot). Raising the count narrows that error with the square root of `N` and costs linearly.

**Reproducibility.** The random fields come from `numpy.random.SeedSequence([SEED, clone, band])` with a fixed `SEED` in `backend/app/uncertainty.py`, so the same Query on the same data gives the same band, to the second, on any machine. A change of method, seed, `ℓ`, clone count or of any DEM or error file changes the cache key.

**Matching the Events.** The nominal Events come from the `/visibility` answer (same Query, same cache). A nominal Event is matched, in each clone, to the clone's Event of the same type that is nearest to it in time. To keep two nominal Events from claiming the same clone Event, the nominal Events of one type cut the Window into cells at the midpoints between consecutive ones, and a clone Event can only match the nominal Event of the cell it falls in; if the cell holds several, the nearest wins and the others are ignored. A clone with no Event of that type in the cell does not have the Event, and `clones_with_event` does not count it: that is the answer to "does it still happen, if the map is a bit different". No time limit is added, because a limit in hours would be wrong for the Earth, whose Events move by days for a change of a few hundredths of a degree, and right for the Sun only by accident.

**Percentiles.** For each Event, the 5th and 95th percentile (linear interpolation between order statistics, `numpy.percentile`'s default, Hyndman and Fan type 7) of the matched times, rounded to the second; `earliest` and `latest` are left out when fewer than half of the clones have the Event. For each metric, the same two percentiles over all the clones. The nominal value is not in the percentiles and can sit at an edge or outside: the glossary says it usually sits at the optimistic end, and the band is where to read how far the pessimistic end goes.

**Background job and cache.** The first request for a Query starts a job and answers 202 with `clones_done` and `clones`; later requests answer 202 until it ends and then 200 from the cache, under the Query's key (Site, Window, Observer height, engine version, fingerprint of the DEM and error files, method version). Each clone takes one engine slot through the gate, politely like the Landing scatter (a visitor already queued goes first, never more than one slot held by a band), and at most one band job runs at a time (`LUNAR_UNCERTAINTY_JOBS`); a job that would have to wait behind a full queue gets 503 busy. The clone Horizon masks do not depend on the Window, so they are kept apart from the answers (720 numbers each) and a second Window on the same Site and Observer height recomputes only the events. The Curated Sites over the default Window are added to the warm-up of ADR 0004, after everything else, taking the warming slot only.

## Considered Options

- **Re-grid synthetic clones from the LOLA points, as Barker et al. did (fractal terrain, orbit perturbations, interpolation).** More faithful, and the only way to get the track-scale errors, but it needs the LOLA point cloud, hours of compute per Site and a method we cannot validate here. Rejected for the engine; the published `_ERR` rasters are the compact form of that study's result.
- **A flat error per DEM (0.3-0.5 m RMS).** Rejected: it ignores that the error is several times larger in sparsely sampled areas, which is where a Site is most uncertain.
- **Fixing the number of clones at 100.** Possible, a setting away; the cost is linear and the 32 already show the band's side and size.

## Consequences

- The band is the map's pixel-scale height error and nothing else. It is not landing error (`/visibility/scatter`), it is not the resolution drop (`/visibility/ladder`), and it does not include the long-wavelength orbit residuals or errors under a pixel (IM-2's 20 m crater is below the finest DEM and no clone of it exists).
- `fixtures/dem/` has no error rasters (they would add tens of MB to the release asset), so tests and the Proof make their own error raster on the fixture grids and say so.
- Off-pole Curated Sites have a zero-width band until `crop_offpole.py` cuts an error product for them (Chandrayaan-3) or one exists (SLDEM2015).
