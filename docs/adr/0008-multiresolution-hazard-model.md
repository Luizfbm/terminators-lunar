# The hazard numbers are Eduardo's cross-validated Monte Carlo on measured laser cells; his planner is an assumption and his swarm an illustration

Status: proposed (Luiz decides)

Eduardo's work (branch `origin/eduardo/cockpit-multirresolucao`, commit 2195fcb, a research workspace with no history in common with `main`) asks one question: from a coarse terrain grid, how much of the ground steeper than a Rover's slope limit at 5 m does each kind of navigation miss? Luiz approved bringing it in on 15/11/2026, contract included (PR 87, tag `hazard` in `contract/openapi.yaml`). This ADR says what enters as a result, what enters as a declared assumption, what is only an illustration and what stays out, and why. It applies ADR 0003's rule to a new question: no terrain invented, no coefficient without a source, and what is Eduardo's choice is called his choice. Computation stays in the backend (ADR 0002): the numbers are precomputed offline and served as stored. `validation/hazard/import_eduardo_bundle.py` copies them from the commit without recomputing them. Files named below are in Eduardo's branch at that commit, except `contract/`, `backend/`, `validation/hazard/import_eduardo_bundle.py`, `GLOSSARY.md` and `docs/frontend-api-guide.md`, which are this repository's.

## What enters as a result: the Monte Carlo (`GET /sites/{site_id}/hazard`)

**The method** (`simulacao_rover/monte_carlo_lola/mc_lola.py`, `tese()`). Normal navigation reads a coarse grid, the mean of 60 m or 20 m blocks interpolated back to 5 m. Multiresolution adds to it the residual between the 5 m height and the coarse grid only on cells with a real laser shot (LDEC > 0), spread by a normalised Gaussian convolution and weighted by ρ/(ρ + 0.02), ρ being the local density of shots, so far from any shot the residual goes to zero and no relief is made up (his rule "No False Relief"). Each cell then gets a margin, the Shield, at the Confidence level P50, P90 or P99 (z of 0, 1.282 and 2.326). It is the same refusal of synthetic terrain as ADR 0003.

**The truth.** Only the 5 m cells with a laser shot count as truth, because the rest of the 5 m product is interpolated too: 7.29% of the cells of the polar cap are measured and 92.71% are not (`CONTEXT.md` §3, from `LDEC_875S_5M`). So the check is a 5-fold cross-validation: in each fold a fifth of the measured cells is hidden from the method and then used to test it. The true slope of each tested cell is drawn 100 times from Normal(slope, slope error), with the error NASA publishes per cell (the `slperr` layer of PGDA product 78, Barker et al. 2021, Planetary and Space Science 203, 105119), seed 2026. Each figure is the mean of the draws with the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles as its 95% interval. Unvetoed hazard and False alarm are the shares defined in `GLOSSARY.md`. This is accepted because it uses only published NASA layers and the error NASA publishes, and it tests on measurements the method did not see.

**Compared at the same False alarm.** A navigation that blocks more ground always misses less of the steep ground, so the Unvetoed hazard of two navigations means nothing unless their False alarm is the same. At AR06 on the 60 m grid (`contract/examples/site-hazard-ar06.example.json`) Normal navigation misses 12.86% with a False alarm of 5.9% and Multiresolution at P90 misses 2.70% with 18.0%: a 79% drop that is mostly the margin. The same-False-alarm curves (`trade_off`, from `mc_lola.py`, section 6c, which sweeps the margin of each navigation over 20 independent draws) give 4.4% against 3.6% at 15% and 2.8% against 2.2% at 20%, about 20% less. Eduardo's own consolidated report says the same of his 26 grids: most of the absolute gain comes from the margin (28.7% to 6.45% on the 60 m grid) and Multiresolution's own gain at the same False alarm is 38.5% in the median, 12% when the coarse grid is 20 m (`docs/Relatorio_Consolidado_Tese_2026.md`, §5.2, table 06). His headline of 79.7% (31.48% to 6.38% over the 26 grids, `docs/PLANO_INTEGRACAO_COCKPIT_TESE.md` §5) compares different False alarms. So a reduction is shown only with the False alarm it was computed at, and the comparison at the same False alarm is the one the screen leads with.

**The margin rule is Eduardo's, partly checked.** The Shield is 1° + z·σ, with σ of 0.8° where the density of shots is at or above the median of the grid's own checked cells and 2.0° elsewhere (`mc_lola.py`, `margem_nominal()`). He gives no source for the 0.8° and 2.0°: the script says only "as in the simulator". He measured it against the SfS terrain of Bertone et al. 2026 in three regions (`sfs_comparacao/entrega_relatorio/RESULTADOS-SFS.md`, 15/11/2026): where cells are interpolated (1° + z·2.0°), P99 was exceeded 0.7% to 1.5% of the time against 1% promised, and P90 3.3% to 6.0% against 10%, so P99 is calibrated and P90 conservative. The report states its limits: SfS is not ground truth and was fitted to the photos used to validate it, three regions, and only interpolated cells were tested. The 0.8° branch, near shots, has no external check. His own calibration on PGDA 78 asks for +2.99° near data at P90 against the nominal +2.03°, and 65% to 75% of the failures that remain are near shots (`Relatorio_Consolidado_Tese_2026.md` §4.2 and §5.2). The labels are the z that was used, not a coverage that was achieved: at AR06 the margin covers 85.4% of cells at P90 and 92.6% at P99 (`coverage` in the same example).

**What the numbers do not say.** Between laser tracks the method does not rebuild the relief. On the 3.3 million cells without a shot in Haworth, against SfS, at a False alarm of 20%, Normal navigation missed 10.3%, Multiresolution 10.4% and the official 5 m map 7.6% (`Relatorio_Consolidado_Tese_2026.md` §5.3.4). The claim that holds is that the margin by density of shots lowers the hazard that passes near shots. The truth cells are the measured ones, 2.4% of the AR06 grid (`measured.grid_fraction`, `cells_checked` 91,471), and `mc_lola.py`'s own report notes that shots sit on rougher ground. The numbers belong to the Hazard grid nearest the Site (`grid.distance_km`), not to its point, and to a slope limit of 15° (`slope_limit_deg`), not to a Rover with another limit. Multiresolution is not better everywhere: at AR06 on the 20 m grid its median error is 0.370° against 0.339° for Normal navigation (Wilcoxon p = 0.15) and the two same-False-alarm curves cross between 15% and 20%. The screen shows what the numbers say.

## What is a declared assumption: the Route (`GET /sites/{site_id}/route`)

The A* planner (`src/js/rover_tese_engine.js`, `planGridAStar` and `simulateRouteComparison`) runs on step costs (1200 plus 200 per degree over the limit for Normal navigation; 7500 plus 900 per degree at 5 m, 520 plus 80 per degree with the margin, for Multiresolution), a heuristic weight of 1.05, a margin weight of 0.55, a speed of 0.35 m/s, a solar efficiency of 0.7, 4% more load per degree above 5° and a veto look-ahead of 75 m. None of these is measured or published. The contract lists every one in `assumptions`, by name, and the screen says they are assumptions. The Rover numbers carry their source and their `estimated` fields (`contract/examples/rovers.example.json`); only VIPER has none estimated. No `source_reference` there gives a slope limit and `estimated` never lists it: his methodology cites 15° for VIPER (Lim 2024) and nothing for the other seven (`docs/Metodologia_Multirresolucao_Fundamentos_e_Plano.md` §3.4), so those limits are his catalogue's. The engine has a third margin rule, σ = max(0.8, 0.35·slope error) or max(2.0, 0.75·slope error) on a density threshold of 0.12; it is declared with the rest and is not the Monte Carlo's.

Two things the Route is not. Its Multiresolution plan reads the 5 m slope of the Hazard grid on every cell, measured or not, and `hazards_hit` is then counted on the same 5 m slope, so it is not tested on cells it did not see as the Monte Carlo is. It shows what a planner with the finer map can avoid, not how many hazards a real drive would meet. And a block of the grid counts as a hazard by its 90th percentile slope at 5 m, so its share of steep ground differs from the share of steep 5 m cells in `/hazard`. At AR06 with VIPER, Normal navigation crosses 11 steep waypoints and Multiresolution 7 (`contract/examples/route-ar06.example.json`).

## What is an illustration: the swarm and the Sun points

`swarm` is 100 agents for 5 generations blended toward the Multiresolution path by a fixed formula (`simulateRouteComparison`), with the first two generations dying on a lowered slope limit. The contract marks it `illustration: true`: its survivor counts are not evidence for either navigation, and in the AR06 example no agent survives in any generation. `solar_points` are spots facing the Sun by a local hillshade rule for the animation, and carry no energy. The screen says so.

## Considered Options

- **The hand-written MLP weights** (`MLP_W1`, `MLP_W2` in `rover_tese_engine.js`, 10 inputs, 6 hidden, 3 outputs): rejected. They are two-decimal numbers presented as "pre-trained", and we found no training script, data or record in the commit that produces them. The trained network in `simulacao_rover/testes/lola_sint/MLP_sint_nom.json` is 8→7→4, learned on synthetic generations, so it is not this one. His own report finds learned models unfit for the terrain coefficients and as a pilot (`Relatorio_Consolidado_Tese_2026.md` §4.4). Not in the contract.
- **The synthetic grid** (`createSyntheticGrid`, sinusoids and made-up craters used when no real grid exists): rejected. A Site with no grid answers `grid: null` or 422 `no_hazard_grid`; fiction is not relief (ADR 0003).
- **The intermediate steps** of `DEGRAUS` (Otimização KKT, Multi 5 m, MC P90): rejected as products. They are rungs of one file with no result of their own in the contract. The app compares two navigations, Normal navigation and Multiresolution (`Navigation` in the contract).
- **Fitted adjustments:** the "+1° on slopes" margin (`Tese P90 + 1° em encosta` in `mc_lola.py`, an "adjustment of the diagnosis" made after looking at the failures, which lifts the False alarm from 17.9% to 23.0%), the log-linear light decay and the mast factor `e^(-0.42(h-1))` (`docs/Metodologia_Multirresolucao_Fundamentos_e_Plano.md`, `Relatorio_Consolidado_Tese_2026.md` §4.2), the 97.7% and 98.3% bench score and the 1 ms edge-computing claims. All are the family ADR 0003 rejected and none is served. His SfS report says the 98.3% was not recomputed after he fixed the engine's lunar scale (`RESULTADOS-SFS.md`, section 4).
- **The rover battery results** (N0 to N3, +16.0% to +37.2% arrival, §5.6 and `docs/PLANO_INTEGRACAO_COCKPIT_TESE.md` §5): rejected for now. They come from a dynamic simulator (`animacao_tese_rover_neuroevolucao_v18.html`) on real strips whose relief was exaggerated 1.3 times for levels N1 to N3 (`Relatorio_Consolidado_Tese_2026.md` §7.2, point 5), with no code in the contract to reproduce them.

## Consequences

- 103 of the 130 Curated Sites have a Hazard grid within 15 km, 27 have none (`backend/app/data/hazard/tiles.json`, `nearest_tile`). For those 27 the screen says there is no 5 m data nearby and shows no number.
- **Three things in the contract do not say what `mc_lola.py` computes, and are for Luiz to change.** `coverage` is described as the share of the truly steep cells a navigation blocks, but the script stores the share of cells whose true slope is at or under the estimate plus the margin (`cobertura_%`), which is what the 85.4% and 92.6% above are. `trade_off` is described as a uniform margin on each navigation, but only Normal navigation's is uniform: Multiresolution's is the margin by density of shots, scaled in size (`FAMILIAS` in `mc_lola.py`; "margem por densidade" in table 07 of his report). And McNemar's `mcnemar_b` and `mcnemar_c` compare Normal navigation without a margin against Multiresolution at P90 with one (`mc_lola.py`, `vg` and `vt`), so that test measures the margin plus the data, not the data. The Wilcoxon on the slope errors has no margin in it, and the same-False-alarm curves are the fair comparison. Until the contract is changed, `docs/frontend-api-guide.md` says how to read both.
- `mean_abs_error_deg` is the same at P50, P90 and P99, as the margin does not move the estimate.
- On screen there are only two navigations, Navegação Normal and Multirresolução (Normal navigation and Multiresolution), and nothing in the product speaks of artificial intelligence.
- Anything new that reads the Query, such as a Route that depends on the Sun at the Cursor, goes through the backend (ADR 0002). The frontend only draws what comes back and interpolates, in a straight line and without leaving the 5% to 30% range, a curve already computed.
